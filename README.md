# Marine-Genomics4
Analysis of Black Band Disease metagenome



A Glimpse Into the World of Marine Genomics:

This module in particular is one that introduces us, students to a variety of core techniques that have been used within the field of marine genomics and marine science, covering everything from the fundamentals of genome expression-based quantitative analyses to metagenomic analysis such as raw sequencing reads and functional interpretation of the genomic structuring of different organisms, by using tools and workflows to understand the segregation and processing of data within the space of modern marine science.


This module progresses through a series of hands-on modules: starting with the introductory command-line fundamentals to the final analysis of a variety of metagenomic functioning. Along the course of the module, key techniques were taught to explore how assembling of DNA sequences into genomes were used for determining their biological functioning while also trying to understand how gene expression varied across organisms and conditions.


The totality of the modules were all ran on the RStudio class server, with detailed step-by-step instructions provided for each session. 


Workshops Covered:
Workshop 1: Introduction to the command line

Workshop 2: Genome assembly

Workshop 3: Metagenome assembly

Workshop 4: Metagenome Function



                                                                                        Analysis of Black Band Disease Occurrence in Corals


 Black Band Disease was first noted across reefs in Belize in the western Caribbean and it was described as an assemblage of microbes which formed bands across healthy, living coral colonies or individuals which moved from one colony to the other, destroying the living and breathing coral tissue leaving behind a dead skeleton . Across the Great Barrier Reef in Australia, the occurrence of BBD is very much prevalent and have been observed across different groups of corals such as Acropora, Montipora, Pocillopora, Brain corals, Pore corals, Goniopora and other soft coral groups. Having an understanding about BBD is relevant and important because of the direct impact it has on coral mortality and the one thing that we will have to realize is the fact that Black Band Disease is not caused by a single microbial group or taxa belonging to a particular species, genus, class or family but is a conjoint congregation of several microbial interactions.
